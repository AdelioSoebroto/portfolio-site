I've decided to spend the next six months building one real project a month, each one deliberately harder than the last. Here's the plan and why it's shaped the way it is.

## Why not just do tutorials

Tutorials are great for learning syntax, but they don't teach you what it feels like when a database migration goes wrong, or why your websocket connection silently drops on mobile. The only way past that is building something with actual moving parts and no answer key.

## The roadmap

1. **Personal Site + Blog** (this project) — Next.js, TypeScript, MDX
2. **Habit/Task Tracker** — full CRUD, real auth, Postgres
3. **Realtime Kanban/Chat Board** — WebSockets, state sync
4. **Order & Inventory System** — Stripe, webhooks, background jobs
5. **AI Document Assistant** — embeddings, vector search, the Claude API
6. **Capstone: Multi-tenant SaaS Dashboard** — everything combined

Full detail on each is on the [projects page](/projects).

## Rules I'm holding myself to

- Ship at "good enough," not "done." Extra polish becomes a backlog item, not a blocker.
- If I'm stuck more than 2-3 days on one bug, that's the signal to get outside help rather than grinding alone.
- Every project gets its own repo, a real README, and a write-up here once it ships.

First stop: finishing this site.
